**[ANSWER]**

The correct answer is Option B.

LLM hallucination occurs because these models are optimized to produce *statistically likely* text — not *factually verified* text. When asked about topics with sparse training data, recent events, or niche facts, the model may generate plausible-sounding but incorrect information, including fake citations, invented statistics, and fabricated quotes from real people.

Ji et al. (2023) conducted a comprehensive survey of hallucination in NLG systems, categorizing types as intrinsic — contradicting source — versus extrinsic — unverifiable content.

For educators, this means AI output must always be treated as a **first draft requiring verification**, never as a primary source. Teaching students to fact-check AI outputs is now a core digital and scientific literacy competency.
