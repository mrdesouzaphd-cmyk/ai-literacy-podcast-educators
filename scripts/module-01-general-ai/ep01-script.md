**[QUESTION]**

Question one.

According to the foundational definition used in computer science and cognitive science, what is Artificial Intelligence?

---

**[ALTERNATIVES]**

Option A — A robot that looks and thinks exactly like a human being.

Option B — Any software program that connects to the internet and processes data.

Option C — The simulation of human intelligence processes by computer systems, including learning, reasoning, and self-correction.

Option D — A database system that stores and retrieves large amounts of information quickly.

---

**[PAUSE CUE]**

Take a moment. Which option do you think is correct?

... three ... two ... one ...

---

**[ANSWER]**

The correct answer is Option C.

Artificial Intelligence refers to the simulation of human intelligence processes by machines — especially computer systems. These processes include learning from data, reasoning toward conclusions, and self-correction over time.

This definition traces back to Alan Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," and was formalized by John McCarthy, who coined the term AI in 1956 at the Dartmouth Conference.

Important note: AI does NOT require a physical robot body. AI can be entirely software-based, running on servers or embedded in apps you already use every day.
